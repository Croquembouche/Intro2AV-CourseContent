    Model assumptions, metrics, and sources
    These are deterministic teaching simulations. Lateral control uses a kinematic bicycle with a 2.7 m wheelbase, fixed speed, 0.05 s time steps, steering angle/rate limits, actuator lag and optional command delay/bias. The plots report rear-axle cross-track error for every lateral controller. Stanley computes its control error at the front axle. Paths are synthetic and there are no obstacles or tire-slip dynamics.
    LQR uses a discrete Riccati iteration at the chosen speed with curvature feedforward. MPC solves a finite-horizon linear error-model quadratic program using 90 ADMM iterations, then enforces a feasible angle/rate sequence. Its predictor omits actuator lag, delay, bias and nonlinear tire dynamics. The displayed residual is the raw QP constraint residual before final feasibility enforcement, not an optimality certificate. MPC does not guarantee stability or road safety here.
    Speed changes with applied acceleration, drag and hill load. Acceleration and braking are limited, and the actuator takes time to respond. Grade starts at 6 s. Derivative acts on filtered speed measurement. Conditional integration implements anti-windup. The braking profile switches the target to 4 m/s at 9 s. Speed feedforward compensates only nominal drag.
    Choose Full run or After 2 s for RMS and peak error. The latter excludes the initial recovery. Every row uses the same selected window; compare runs with equal duration. Actuator limiting is the percentage of samples where requested steering exceeds the angle/rate envelope (or acceleration saturates). CSV values use radians for steering and m/s² for acceleration. A fair comparison keeps the scenario fixed and reports weights/gains as well as the metrics. Preset tuning is illustrative, not an equal-effort benchmark.
