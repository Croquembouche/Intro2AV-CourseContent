How a vehicle follows a plan

This guide accompanies the lecture demonstrations. Watch the motion, compare two cases, and explain the difference in words. No calculations, written exercises, or exported results are required.

Feedback

Look, adjust, and look again.

A command changes the vehicle’s situation. Feedback uses the next measurement to correct what remains. Feedforward anticipates a known demand using a model; feedback helps with the difference between prediction and reality.

PID: react, remember, damp

P responds to the present speed gap. I builds correction for a gap that persists. D damps rapid changes in measured speed.

  • Compare P on a hill with PI on a hill: watch the persistent gap close when integral action is added.
  • Compare the two braking cases: stored correction can delay recovery when the actuator is limited. Anti-windup pauses buildup that makes this worse.

Pure Pursuit: aim ahead

The orange target moves along the path. The vehicle turns toward it, moves, and chooses a new target.

Compare Short lookahead and Long lookahead. A near target usually brings sharper corrections; a far target can make steering gentler but cut corners. Watch both the target and the path the vehicle actually follows.

Stanley: align and move toward the path

Stanley considers the direction the car points and the sideways gap at the front axle. A car can be close to a path while pointed away from it.

Compare Strong gain with Strong gain + delay. A correction can arrive after the situation has changed, producing repeated swings.

LQR: choose a tradeoff with a model

LQR uses a simplified model to design a feedback rule. Tracking priority favors smaller error; steering penalty discourages large corrections.

Compare Tracking priority with Steering penalty, then try Limited actuator. A preference for smaller commands is different from a hard physical limit.

Watch LQR

MPC: predict, act once, replan

The green path is a prediction. The orange first action is the next move. The rest can change.

Compare Short horizon and Long horizon, then the tight-limit cases. More preview reveals more of the path, but a longer plan is not automatically better. A new measurement can reveal that the model was wrong, so the controller updates its plan.

Choosing a controller

Compare Baseline and Added delay. Look for closeness to the path, a large miss, repeated swings, and steering activity. No single score or default ranking describes every situation.

These demonstrations use simplified vehicle models. They do not include obstacles, road boundaries, or tire slip. Successful tracking here is not proof of real-vehicle safety.
